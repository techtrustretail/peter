Unit 1
Living Things

My body, animals
& staying healthy

Cơ thể, động vật & sức khoẻ

1🦴 Bones & skeleton (1.1)

  • A skeleton = a strong frame of bones that supports the body from the inside. (bộ xương)
  • Humans have 206 bones of different sizes and shapes.
  • Bones are hard and strong but light → animals can move easily.
  • Skeletons of dinosaurs tell us about animals that lived long ago 🦕.
💀 skull sọ😬 jaw hàm🐍 spine cột sống🫁 rib cage lồng ngực🦵 hip hông💪 upper / lower arm bones🦿 thigh bone, lower leg bones
  • A model helps us see how something looks or works. A model is not the same as the real thing (e.g. a straw skeleton cannot move or grow).

2🛡️ Why we need a skeleton (1.2)

Job (function)How
1. Protect organsskull → brain 🧠 · rib cage → heart ❤️ & lungs 🫁
2. Support & give shapeholds the body up, we can't squash it
3. Help us movemuscles are joined to bones
4. Help us growwe get bigger because our skeleton grows; an adult's has stopped
  • Organs = parts inside the body that keep us alive. (cơ quan)
  • Measure bones with a tape measure in cm (standard unit). Hand lengths are not good — everyone's hand is a different size.
Baby 5 cm → Gita 11 cm → sister 18 cm. Older = longer foot, so an 18-year-old brother ≈ 25 cm.

3💪 Muscles & movement (1.3)

  • Bones cannot bend. Muscles (under the skin, joined to bones) make us move.
  • Muscles pull bones — they never push.
  • Muscles always work in pairs.
contracts co lạirelaxes duỗi ra
gets shorter & fatter → pulls the bonegets longer & thinner → back to normal size, rests
Arm lifts: front muscle (biceps) contracts, back muscle (triceps) relaxes. Arm drops: the opposite.
  • Moving keeps us healthy: heart & lungs work better, stronger muscles & bones, think better, good mood 😊. Sitting still too long is not good.

4🐟🦗 Different skeletons (1.4)

Vertebrates = animals with a backbone, skeleton inside. 5 groups:

🐟 fishlive in water, fins, scales
🐸 amphibianswater & land, smooth wet skin
🦎 reptilesdry scales (snake, lizard, crocodile)
🦅 birdsfeathers, wings
🐱 mammalshair or fur (dolphin, whale too!)

Invertebrates = no backbone:

  • Exoskeleton = hard skin/shell on the outside; protects & supports: 🦗 locust, grasshopper, 🪲 beetle, 🦀 crab, 🕷️ spider
  • Soft body, no exoskeleton: 🪱 earthworm, 🪼 jellyfish, 🐌 snail
  • Identification key = yes/no questions that lead to the animal's name.

5💊 Medicines & diseases (1.5)

  • Medicines make us better when ill; some prevent illness.
  • Safely: only from a doctor, nurse or your adult · read & follow the instructions · right amount (too much is harmful) · never take a friend's medicine · don't take extra if you forget.
  • Ways: 💧 liquid / tablets · 💉 injection · 🌬️ inhaler (asthma) · 🧴 cream (skin, insect bites) · 🏥 drip (straight into the blood in hospital)
  • Infectious disease = caused by tiny living things called germs that infect (get into) the body.
  • People (flu, measles, malaria), animals (🐔 bird flu) and plants (🌾 leaf blast on rice) can all get them.
  • Vaccination = injection that prevents a disease. Farmers vaccinate chickens against bird flu and wear gloves.

Unit 2
Energy

Energy makes things
move or change

Năng lượng làm vật chuyển động hoặc thay đổi

1🌈 Energy around us (2.1)

Forms of energy (các dạng năng lượng):

💡 light🔥 heat🔊 sound🏃 movement🔌 electrical
  • Energy makes things move, change or work.
  • The Sun ☀️ is a source of energy — it is not a form of energy.
  • We cannot make energy and it does not disappear. It can only move or change form.
  • Things that are still can have energy stored (food, a battery, a log of wood).
  • All living things need energy.

2➡️ Energy transfers (2.2)

  • Transfer = energy moves from one thing to another. (truyền năng lượng)
  • An energy chain uses arrows to show where energy goes.
☀️Sun →👕wet clothes →water dries
♨️hot water →🥄spoon →petroleum jelly →🔴bead falls
✋warm hand →🧊ice melts
  • Some heat always escapes to the surroundings (air around) → that's why hot water cools down.
  • Transfers: kicking a ball ⚽, hitting a drum 🥁, Sun drying clothes. A log lying still = not a transfer.

Energy can change form when it is transferred:

4🌿 Energy & living things (2.4)

  • Plants use energy from the Sun to make their own food → producers.
  • Animals cannot make food; they eat plants or animals → consumers.
  • A food chain always starts with a plant. Arrow → = "is eaten by" (energy goes this way).
🌿plant →🐇rabbit →🦅eagle

Unit 3
Materials

Everything is made of
tiny particles

Mọi thứ đều được tạo từ hạt rất nhỏ

1🧱 Materials, substances, particles (3.1)

  • Matter = everything around us.
  • Material = a type of matter; most are mixtures: wood, glass, plastic, rubber.
  • Substance = one pure solid, liquid or gas, not a mixture: salt 🧂, pure water 💧, oxygen.
  • Particle = a very tiny bit. All matter is made of particles. Most are too small to see. They are always moving and have spaces between them. (hạt)
Glass is a material: sand + soda ash + limestone are heated → liquid glass → shaped → cools and goes hard.
Drops of food dye spread in still water → water particles move!

2🔵 The particle model (3.1–3.2)

SolidLiquid
packed tightly in a regular patternclose but no pattern
very small spacesbigger spaces
particles stay in a fixed placeparticles slide past each other
keeps its shape unless pushed or squeezedflows, can be poured, takes the shape of its container, spreads out on a flat surface
  • A property describes what a material is like or how it behaves.

3🏖️ Solids that pour (3.2)

  • Powders — sand, flour, salt, sugar — are solids that can pour and take the shape of their container, like a liquid.
  • Why? They are lots of tiny grains with air (space) between them, so the grains can move and flow past each other.
  • But each grain is still a solid made of millions of particles.
Time 100 ml of each powder flowing through a funnel. Look for a pattern between grain size and speed, then use it to predict a new powder.
  • Reliable results = repeat the test and get the same or similar answer.
  • Use standard units (seconds) so you can compare with other groups.

4🍫 Melting & solidifying (3.3)

🧊solid — heating 🔥 → melting → liquid💧
💧liquid — cooling ❄️ → solidifying → solid🧊
  • This is a change of state. Solidifying is also called freezing.
  • Heating: particles gain energy → move faster and further apart → solid melts.
  • Cooling: particles lose energy → move slower and closer → liquid solidifies.
  • Change of state is a physical process: the substance stays the same (ice and water are both water) and can change back.
  • Different solids melt at different speeds (🧊 ice, 🍫 chocolate, 🧈 butter). Metals like gold need very high temperatures.
Ice cream: liquid mix 🥛 → freezer ❄️ → solid ice cream 🍦 = solidifying.

5🧪 Chemical reactions (3.4)

  • Some substances react together and make a NEW substance = chemical reaction. Most cannot be changed back.
  • 🔥 Burning wood: wood + air → ash + gases
  • 🧱 Cement + sand + water + limestone → concrete
  • 🔩 Rusting: iron/steel + water + air (oxygen) → reddish-brown rust
  • Rust makes metal weak → damages cars, bridges, buildings.
  • Stop rust: paint 🎨, oil or grease 🛢️, plastic coating → keeps water and air away.
Wire wool in: water only (covered, lid) · water + air · air only. Rust is quickest with water + air. Oil on water stops air → rust is slower.
